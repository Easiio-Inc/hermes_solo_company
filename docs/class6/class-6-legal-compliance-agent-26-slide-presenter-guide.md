# AI Solo Company Class 6 — 26-Slide Presenter Guide

**Class:** AI Legal & Compliance Agent  
**Chinese title:** 第 6 课：AI 法律与合规 Agent  
**Teaching boundary:** This lesson teaches first-pass review support, not final legal advice.  
**Recommended length:** 90 minutes

---

## Slide 1 — Class 6: AI Legal & Compliance Agent
**On-slide copy**
- Review contracts, privacy policies, and terms of service with AI
- Escalate high-risk issues to human review

**Speaker notes**
- Today we are not building an AI lawyer.
- We are building a repeatable compliance review workflow.
- The goal is to help founders detect risk early, organize findings, and know when to escalate.

**Visual direction**
- Dark title slide
- Horizontal flow: Document → Checklist → Risk Flags → Human Review → Summary

---

## Slide 2 — Where this class fits
**On-slide copy**
- Earlier: website, lead capture, skills, finance workflows
- Today: legal/compliance review workflow
- Later: operations, sales, support, growth systems

**Speaker notes**
- The class sequence matters.
- Students already know how to attract leads and structure workflows.
- Now we add a risk-control layer so growth is not disconnected from compliance.

**Visual direction**
- Timeline or journey bar with Class 6 highlighted

---

## Slide 3 — The founder problem
**On-slide copy**
- Client agreements
- Contractor agreements
- Privacy policies
- Terms of service
- Refund and cancellation rules
- Data disclosures

**Speaker notes**
- Founders sign or publish these documents constantly.
- The common problem is not only writing them; it is knowing what is missing, risky, or one-sided.
- This is where AI can help as a first-pass reviewer.

**Visual direction**
- 2-column slide: left = document list, right = warning/risk icons

---

## Slide 4 — Why compliance matters for AI solo companies
**On-slide copy**
- Unclear payment terms hurt revenue
- One-sided liability creates exposure
- Weak privacy promises create trust risk
- Website policies can conflict with real operations

**Speaker notes**
- A fast-moving solo company often automates growth before it hardens policy.
- That creates hidden business risk.
- If the legal layer is weak, the whole company stack is fragile.

**Visual direction**
- 4 risk cards with icons: money, shield, privacy, website

---

## Slide 5 — What the AI agent should do
**On-slide copy**
- Run checklist review
- Scan clauses for obvious risk
- Detect missing sections
- Summarize issues clearly
- Prepare human review packets

**Speaker notes**
- This is the productive zone for AI.
- AI is very good at structured scanning, pattern matching, and creating readable summaries.
- Those are exactly the jobs we want here.

**Visual direction**
- Five-card grid with green accent

---

## Slide 6 — What the AI agent should never claim
**On-slide copy**
- Not a lawyer
- Not final legal advice
- Not a signoff authority
- Not automatically jurisdiction-safe

**Speaker notes**
- Students must internalize this boundary.
- If the AI sounds too confident, that is a danger signal.
- In this workflow, uncertainty should lead to escalation, not pretend certainty.

**Visual direction**
- Red warning card on right side, “First-pass only” badge

---

## Slide 7 — Today’s deliverable
**On-slide copy**
- Build a compliance checking workflow
- Review one sample document
- Flag risks by severity
- Escalate high-risk items
- Produce a structured review summary

**Speaker notes**
- The output for this class is not just a prompt.
- It is a repeatable workflow that can be reused across the business.
- In Chinese, the deliverable is 合规检查流程.

**Visual direction**
- Large central outcome card with 5 supporting steps

## Slide 8 — The core workflow
**On-slide copy**
1. Document intake
2. Document type detection
3. Checklist selection
4. Clause scan
5. Risk flagging
6. Missing-section detection
7. Human review escalation
8. Compliance summary

**Speaker notes**
- This is the backbone of the lesson.
- Everything we do today maps back to this flow.
- Students should leave able to explain this sequence in order.

**Visual direction**
- Process pipeline with numbered steps

## Slide 9 — Three core document families
**On-slide copy**
- Contracts / agreements
- Privacy policy
- Terms of service

**Speaker notes**
- These are the most common legal surfaces for an AI solo company.
- Together they cover customer obligations, data handling, and platform/business rules.

**Visual direction**
- 3-column comparison with examples under each column

---

## Slide 10 — What we review in contracts
**On-slide copy**
- Scope of work
- Payment terms
- Cancellation / termination
- IP ownership
- Confidentiality
- Liability limits
- Dispute / governing law

**Speaker notes**
- A contract review is not only about reading words.
- It is about checking whether key business protections are present and balanced.
- Missing payment or liability structure is often more important than surface wording.

**Visual direction**
- Contract checklist card with checkbox motif

---

## Slide 11 — What we review in privacy policies
**On-slide copy**
- Data collected
- Purpose of collection
- Cookies / tracking
- Third-party sharing
- User rights / contact path
- Retention / deletion
- Security statements

**Speaker notes**
- This is where website operations meet compliance.
- A policy should match what the company actually does, not what sounds nice.
- Vague sharing or retention language is a common risk area.

**Visual direction**
- Privacy/data card grid with data icons

---

## Slide 12 — What we review in terms of service
**On-slide copy**
- Acceptable use
- Subscription / renewal rules
- Refund policy
- Suspension / termination
- Liability limitation
- Dispute resolution
- Update / notice process

**Speaker notes**
- Terms of service define the operating rules between the company and the user.
- This is where recurring billing and unilateral suspension risks often show up.

**Visual direction**
- Platform rules layout with user/company split view

---

## Slide 13 — Why checklist review matters
**On-slide copy**
- Reduces random prompting
- Forces consistency
- Helps compare documents
- Exposes missing sections quickly

**Speaker notes**
- Without a checklist, students will ask vague questions and get vague answers.
- The checklist is what turns a chatbot interaction into an operational workflow.

**Visual direction**
- Before/after comparison: random prompt vs structured review

---

## Slide 14 — Our 3-level risk rubric
**On-slide copy**
- Low risk
- Medium risk
- High risk

**Speaker notes**
- We use a simple rubric on purpose.
- Complex scoring systems look smart but are harder to teach and harder to trust.
- Simple, explainable risk labels are more useful in class and in operations.

**Visual direction**
- Three stacked color bands: green, amber, red

---

## Slide 15 — What low risk looks like
**On-slide copy**
- Minor wording cleanup
- Formatting issue
- Small missing detail
- Low-impact clarification

**Speaker notes**
- Low risk does not mean irrelevant.
- It means the issue is unlikely to materially change legal or business exposure by itself.

**Visual direction**
- Green examples card

---

## Slide 16 — What medium risk looks like
**On-slide copy**
- Ambiguous payment timing
- Weak refund wording
- Unclear ownership language
- Incomplete privacy disclosure

**Speaker notes**
- Medium risk often means the clause could create confusion, dispute, or operational misalignment.
- These items usually require revision, and sometimes human review depending on context.

**Visual direction**
- Amber examples card

---

## Slide 17 — What high risk looks like
**On-slide copy**
- Unlimited liability
- One-sided indemnity
- Broad IP transfer
- Missing privacy disclosures
- Unilateral suspension rights
- Missing dispute structure

**Speaker notes**
- High risk means material legal, financial, or operational impact is possible.
- These items should be escalated, not casually rewritten and ignored.

**Visual direction**
- Red alert cards with strong contrast

---

## Slide 18 — When AI must escalate
**On-slide copy**
- High risk → human review
- Liability / privacy / IP / dispute rights affected → human review
- Minor wording only → revise internally first

**Speaker notes**
- This is the decision gate that keeps the workflow safe.
- When in doubt, the system should prefer flag for review over false confidence.

**Visual direction**
- Decision tree or routing diagram

---

## Slide 23 — What good output looks like
**On-slide copy**
- Document type
- Summary
- Flagged clauses
- Risk level
- Missing sections / facts
- Human review required
- Next actions

**Speaker notes**
- A good output is readable, structured, and actionable.
- The best AI review outputs do not just point at problems; they help route next steps.

**Visual direction**
- Table or JSON-style structured card

---

## Slide 24 — What to send to a human reviewer
**On-slide copy**
- Exact clause text
- Document type
- AI risk label
- Why it may be risky
- Unknown facts / ambiguity
- Suggested next reviewer

**Speaker notes**
- Escalation quality is critical.
- A vague handoff wastes the reviewer’s time.
- A good packet makes the next human decision faster and safer.

**Visual direction**
- Handoff packet card / checklist

---

## Slide 25 — Student exercise
**On-slide copy**
- Choose one document type
- Run checklist review
- List top 3 risks
- Identify missing sections
- Mark human review items
- Write next actions

**Speaker notes**
- This can be done individually or in pairs.
- If time is short, assign one document family per group and compare findings.

**Visual direction**
- Exercise worksheet card with numbered tasks

---

## Slide 26 — Final takeaway
**On-slide copy**
- We are not building an AI lawyer
- We are building a repeatable compliance workflow
- AI helps detect and organize risk early
- Humans approve high-risk decisions

**Speaker notes**
- End by reinforcing the workflow mindset.
- The real win is not one good answer.
- The win is a repeatable process that makes the company safer and more systematic.

**Visual direction**
- Dark closing slide with strong final statement

## Presenter pacing suggestion
- Slides 1–4: 10–12 min
- Slides 5–9: 12–15 min
- Slides 10–18: 25–30 min
- Slides 19–22: 20–25 min
- Slides 23–26: 10–12 min

## Design suggestion
- Title/closing: dark background
- Content slides: light background
- Use one accent system throughout:
  - green = low risk
  - amber = medium risk
  - red = high risk
- Use shapes, cards, and arrows instead of dense paragraphs
