# Small Solo Company Legal Playbook Example

> Purpose: starter playbook for a small AI/consulting/software solo company. This is a business template for first-pass review, not final legal advice.

## Company profile assumptions

- Company is a small solo operator or tiny team.
- Company sells lightweight AI automation, consulting, implementation, website/chatbot setup, training, or software-enabled services.
- Company wants to avoid enterprise-style legal exposure that is too heavy for a small operator.

## Preferred commercial positions

### Payment terms
- Upfront deposit preferred for project work.
- Standard invoice payment target: net 7 or net 14.
- Do not accept long payment windows like net 45 or net 60 unless pricing supports it.
- For subscriptions, billing cadence and renewal date must be explicit.

### Refunds / credits
- Custom project deposits are generally non-refundable once work starts.
- Prepaid subscriptions may offer prorated credits only if explicitly approved.
- Do not promise open-ended refunds.

### Renewals
- Auto-renew may be acceptable only if:
  - renewal cadence is clearly stated
  - customer gets advance notice
  - cancellation method is clear
- Avoid auto-renew clauses with no notice or no easy exit.

### Scope changes
- Out-of-scope work should require written approval or change order.
- Avoid open-ended obligations to perform undefined future work.

## IP positions

### Pre-existing IP
- Company keeps ownership of pre-existing tools, templates, prompts, know-how, code libraries, and reusable systems.
- Customer receives only the license or deliverables explicitly granted in the agreement.

### Client deliverables
- Client may own final paid-for custom deliverables, except company background IP and reusable components.
- Company should retain the right to reuse general know-how, ideas, workflows, and non-confidential learnings.

### Open source / third-party tools
- Agreement should allow use of third-party tools, APIs, models, plugins, and open-source components under their own licenses.

## Data / privacy positions

- Company should not promise unrealistic security guarantees.
- Company should avoid language that makes it responsible for all third-party AI/model/provider behavior.
- If customer data is used, agreement should state the intended purpose and any third-party processors that materially matter.
- Avoid broad promises like "we will never use any subcontractor or third-party service" if that is not operationally true.

## Liability positions

### Liability cap
- Strong preference: total liability capped at fees paid in the last 3 to 12 months, or total fees paid under the agreement.
- Unlimited liability is a high-risk deviation for a solo company.

### Excluded damages
- Prefer exclusion of indirect, consequential, special, incidental, and lost-profit damages.

### Indemnity
- Avoid broad one-way indemnity in favor of the counterparty.
- If indemnity exists, it should be narrow and tied to specific breaches or infringement claims.
- Avoid indemnifying for customer misuse, customer data, or customer-provided content unless clearly limited.

## Term / termination positions

- Either party should have a clear termination path.
- Company should be able to suspend or terminate for non-payment, abuse, legal risk, or security risk.
- Customer should pay for work completed through the termination date.
- Avoid clauses locking the company into long terms with no exit for non-payment or scope abuse.

## Confidentiality positions

- Mutual confidentiality is preferred.
- Confidential information should exclude public info, already-known info, independently developed info, and legally compelled disclosures.

## Dispute positions

- Prefer practical, simple dispute language.
- Avoid expensive or remote forums that are unrealistic for a solo company.
- Governing law and venue should ideally match the company's home base or a neutral practical venue.

## Red-flag clauses for this playbook

Treat these as likely high-risk unless heavily narrowed:
- unlimited liability
- broad one-way indemnity
- automatic IP assignment of all tools/workflows/background IP
- auto-renew with no notice or no cancellation path
- payment terms longer than 30 days for small project work
- non-cancellable long-term commitments with no protective exit
- broad security/compliance warranties beyond actual operations
- liability for all third-party tools, models, or customer content

## Recommended fallback redlines

### Liability cap fallback
- "Company's total liability will not exceed the fees paid by Customer under this Agreement in the 12 months before the claim."

### Indemnity fallback
- "Each party will indemnify the other only for third-party claims arising from its own gross negligence, willful misconduct, or infringement caused by materials it supplied."

### Background IP fallback
- "Company retains ownership of all pre-existing materials, tools, templates, know-how, and reusable components, while Customer receives the rights expressly granted to the final deliverables."

### Renewal fallback
- "Any renewal must include advance notice and Customer may cancel before the renewal date through a clearly described process."

### Termination fallback
- "Either party may terminate for material breach after notice and cure period, and Company may suspend or terminate for non-payment, abuse, or legal/security risk."

## How to use this playbook in review

For each important clause, classify it as:
- aligned
- acceptable with note
- outside playbook
- high-risk deviation
- missing key protection

Then recommend:
- accept
- accept with note
- redline
- escalate to human legal review
