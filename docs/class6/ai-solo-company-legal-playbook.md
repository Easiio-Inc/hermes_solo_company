# AI Solo Company Legal Playbook Example

> Purpose: a classroom example legal playbook for a small AI solo company that sells website builds, chatbot setup, AI workflow automation, training, and support. This is first-pass review support, not final legal advice.

## 1. Company profile

- **Business model:** small founder-led AI services + software-enabled implementation company
- **Typical offers:** website/chatbot setup, AI workflow integration, training, support retainers, lightweight software modules
- **Risk posture:** avoid enterprise-style legal exposure that is too large for a solo or tiny team
- **Teaching goal:** show students how a company can compare incoming legal language against its own preferred fallback positions

## 2. Non-negotiable review boundaries

- AI review is only a **first-pass review support** workflow.
- Any **high-risk deviation**, missing mandatory protection, or ambiguous language must go to **human legal review**.
- A clause can match the playbook and still require human review if the transaction is large, cross-border, regulated, or strategically important.

## 3. Preferred commercial positions

### Payment terms
- Project work should use **30%–50% upfront deposit** when possible.
- Standard invoice target is **net 7** or **net 14**.
- Avoid payment windows longer than **net 30** unless pricing clearly compensates for the cash-flow risk.
- Customer may not withhold undisputed payment because of minor delay or minor non-material issue.

### Refunds and credits
- Custom implementation deposits become non-refundable once work starts.
- Subscription/service credits should be limited and explicit.
- Avoid open-ended promises such as “full refund at any time for any dissatisfaction.”

### Scope change control
- Any out-of-scope request needs written approval, revised estimate, or change order.
- Avoid language that forces unlimited revisions, undefined support, or open-ended strategic work.

### Term and renewal
- Auto-renew is acceptable only when notice, billing cadence, and cancellation path are clearly stated.
- Avoid silent renewal with no reminder or no practical exit.
- For recurring support, either party should have a clear termination path after notice.

## 4. IP and deliverable positions

### Background IP
- Company keeps ownership of pre-existing prompts, templates, workflows, reusable code, know-how, internal agent systems, and training materials.
- Customer only receives rights expressly granted for the final deliverables.

### Client deliverables
- Paid custom deliverables may transfer or license to the customer as defined in the agreement.
- Company retains the right to reuse general ideas, know-how, non-confidential patterns, and background systems.

### Third-party tools
- Agreements must allow reasonable use of third-party APIs, AI models, open-source tools, plugins, and hosting providers under their own terms.
- Company should not guarantee that every third-party service will be continuously available or error-free.

## 5. Data and privacy positions

- Do not promise absolute security or zero-risk security language.
- If personal data is handled, the agreement or policy should describe the intended purpose, main processors, and retention logic at a practical level.
- Avoid promising that the company never uses subcontractors or third-party services if that is not operationally true.
- Avoid taking responsibility for all customer-provided content, data quality, or unlawful customer instructions.

## 6. Liability positions

### Liability cap
- Strong preference: total liability capped at **fees paid in the prior 12 months** or **total fees paid under the agreement**.
- **Unlimited liability** is a high-risk deviation.

### Excluded damages
- Prefer exclusion of indirect, incidental, consequential, special, and lost-profit damages.

### Indemnity
- Avoid broad one-way indemnity for the other side.
- If indemnity exists, it should be narrow and tied to specific breaches, IP infringement, or misconduct caused by the indemnifying party.
- Do not indemnify the other side for their misuse, their data, or their instructions unless tightly limited.

## 7. Operational protection positions

- Company may suspend work for **non-payment**, **security risk**, **abusive behavior**, or **legal/compliance risk**.
- Customer remains responsible for fees for work completed through termination.
- Any SLA or uptime language must reflect actual support capacity.
- Avoid warranties that imply the AI output is guaranteed accurate, legally compliant, or suitable for every purpose.

## 8. Dispute and governing-law positions

- Prefer practical and cost-sensitive dispute language.
- Avoid remote or expensive venues unrealistic for a solo company.
- Governing law should ideally match the company home base or another practical neutral venue.

## 9. Mandatory protections checklist

Treat these as **missing key protection** if absent where relevant:

- liability cap
- payment timing
- termination / suspension rights
- background IP reservation
- third-party tools permission
- confidentiality baseline
- change-control or scope boundary language
- basic data/privacy purpose language when data handling is involved

## 10. Playbook alignment labels

Use these labels during review:

- **aligned**
- **acceptable_with_note**
- **outside_playbook**
- **high_risk_deviation**
- **missing_key_protection**

## 11. High-risk escalation triggers

Escalate when the document includes:

- unlimited liability
- broad one-way indemnity
- assignment of all company tools, prompts, code, or workflow IP
- customer right to withhold payment broadly
- no exit for non-payment or abusive client behavior
- privacy promises beyond actual operations
- full responsibility for third-party AI/model/provider behavior
- silent auto-renewal without clear notice/cancellation
- no practical limitation on revisions or scope growth

## 12. Recommended fallback redlines

### Liability cap fallback
> Company’s aggregate liability will not exceed the fees paid under this Agreement in the 12 months before the claim.

### Background IP fallback
> Company retains ownership of all pre-existing materials, prompts, templates, know-how, reusable code, systems, and tools used to provide the Services.

### Payment fallback
> Customer will pay undisputed invoices within 14 days. Company may suspend work for overdue undisputed amounts after written notice.

### Indemnity fallback
> Each party will indemnify the other only for third-party claims arising from its own gross negligence, willful misconduct, or infringement caused by materials it supplied.

### Scope control fallback
> Requests outside the agreed scope require written approval, timeline review, and any applicable fee adjustment before work begins.

### Renewal fallback
> Any renewal must state the renewal term, pricing, notice window, and cancellation method.

## 13. How students should use this playbook

1. Identify the document family: contract, privacy, or terms.
2. Match the clause to the relevant playbook topic.
3. Quote the risky or important clause.
4. Mark whether it is aligned, acceptable with note, outside playbook, high-risk deviation, or missing key protection.
5. Explain why it matters in business terms.
6. Add a suggested follow-up or redline.
7. Escalate high-risk or uncertain items to human review.
