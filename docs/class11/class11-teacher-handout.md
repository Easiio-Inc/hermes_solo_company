# Class 11 Teacher Handout

Use this handout to run a live Class 11 session with a clear teaching flow, evidence standards, and review-first decision points.

---

## Class goal
By the end of Class 11, students should be able to:
- define a market clearly before researching it
- collect or paste usable customer and competitor evidence
- identify repeated customer pain points
- compare competitors and find underserved gaps
- rank opportunities with a simple rubric
- recommend one niche to test first

---

## Core teaching principle
Do not start with market hype.

Start with:
1. market definition
2. evidence quality
3. pain patterns
4. competitor gaps
5. opportunity shortlist
6. one clear recommendation

This prevents students from jumping too early into broad business ideas with weak evidence.

---

## Recommended live class flow

### Part 1 — Define the market clearly
Ask students to state:
- the business topic
- the target audience
- the market goal
- known competitors
- any founder constraints

Teaching point:
A market that is too broad will produce generic answers.

### Part 2 — Collect and inspect evidence
Ask students to bring 3 to 5 evidence blocks such as:
- customer reviews
- Reddit or forum posts
- social comments
- competitor website copy
- pricing notes
- customer interview notes

Teaching point:
The first workflow is only as good as the evidence quality.

### Part 3 — Run the Class 11 workflow
Explain the module order:
1. pain-point analysis
2. competitor gap analysis
3. opportunity generation
4. opportunity scoring
5. final report

Student tracked skill copy:
- `docs/class11/class11-market-research-agent/SKILL.md`

### Part 4 — Review the output critically
Ask:
- which pains repeat most often?
- which competitor gap is most credible?
- which opportunity is narrow enough to test?
- what makes the recommended niche stronger than the others?

### Part 5 — End with one practical next step
Examples:
- validate with 5 customer interviews
- test outreach with one niche landing page
- build a narrow MVP around one workflow pain

---

## What to emphasize while teaching

### 1. Evidence beats excitement
Students often start with a broad idea they like.
Correct framing:
- start with market signals
- then narrow to one workable niche

### 2. Pain patterns matter more than isolated opinions
One dramatic quote is less valuable than repeated evidence.

### 3. The best answer is usually narrower than students expect
Encourage students to move from a broad market to one segment with one repeated pain and one plausible offer.

---

## Sample instructor prompts

### Prompt 1 — First-pass analysis
```text
Use the Class 11 market research workflow.
Return:
- market brief summary
- evidence quality note
- pain-point analysis
- competitor gap analysis
- opportunity shortlist
- recommended niche
- best next validation step
```

### Prompt 2 — Compare two niche directions
```text
Based on the existing Class 11 output, compare the top two opportunity options.
Explain which one is better for a fast MVP and why.
```

### Prompt 3 — Make the next step concrete
```text
Take the recommended niche and turn it into a 7-day validation plan.
Keep it realistic for a solo founder or student.
```

---

## Common mistakes to correct in class
- using evidence that is too thin or too vague
- analyzing a market that is too broad
- confusing competitor messaging with actual customer demand
- listing many ideas without choosing one
- ending with a generic recommendation and no next step
