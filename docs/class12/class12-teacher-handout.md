# Class 12 Teacher Handout

Use this handout to run a live Class 12 session with a clear teaching flow, lead-handling rules, and review-first sales discipline.

---

## Class goal
By the end of Class 12, students should be able to:
- distinguish customer-service inquiries from sales-qualified opportunities
- score leads consistently
- draft consultative follow-up in email, DM, and call-script formats
- prepare CRM-ready notes and stage updates
- recommend one clear next action for each lead

---

## Core teaching principle
Do not start with automated closing.

Start with:
1. summarize the inquiry
2. classify the route
3. qualify the lead
4. draft the response
5. update the CRM handoff
6. recommend the next step

This keeps the workflow practical, safe, and useful for a small business owner or student operator.

---

## Recommended live class flow

### Part 1 — Explain the conversion pipeline
Teach the Class 12 flow:
- chatbot or website lead
- qualification
- response draft
- CRM note
- follow-up task
- optional quote or proposal next step

### Part 2 — Explain service vs sales
Ask students to identify whether each example is:
- customer service only
- sales-qualified lead
- weak-fit or nurture lead

Teaching point:
Not every inbound message should receive the same sales response.

### Part 3 — Run lead scoring
Use the qualification skill to evaluate:
- fit
- urgency
- budget
- timeline
- buying intent

### Part 4 — Draft the response package
For a qualified lead, return:
- qualification score
- email follow-up
- DM follow-up
- call script
- best CTA

### Part 5 — Prepare the CRM handoff
Turn the conversation into:
- CRM summary
- stage recommendation
- follow-up task
- operator next action

---

## What to emphasize while teaching

### 1. Helpful first, sales second
A small business should sound practical and trustworthy, not scripted or aggressive.

### 2. Qualification saves time
Students should learn that scoring and routing reduce wasted effort.

### 3. Every lead needs one next action
A lead without a next action is not part of a real workflow.

---

## Common mistakes to correct in class
- treating all leads as high priority
- replying with generic AI hype instead of business-specific help
- skipping qualification and jumping straight to quoting
- forgetting to capture the next step in the CRM handoff
- confusing a service question with a real buying signal
