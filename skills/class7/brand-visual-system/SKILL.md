# Brand Visual System

## When to use

Use this skill when the user wants to turn a business, offer, or founder positioning into a usable brand system instead of vague design exploration.

Typical use cases:

- Class 7 AI Solo Company lesson work
- founder brand positioning for a new website
- converting a business brief into visual direction
- generating prompt-ready creative specs for hero, logo, social, or promo assets
- mapping brand outputs back into homepage implementation

## Core outcome

Produce **one selected direction** that can actually be applied to a website.

Do not stop at style brainstorming. The goal is:

1. understand the business
2. write a compact brand brief
3. generate 3 distinct directions
4. choose 1 winner
5. build prompts and design rules from the winner
6. map the winner into homepage and social implementation

## Required inputs

Collect or infer these when possible:

- business name
- offer / product / service
- target audience
- desired tone
- differentiation or positioning angle
- constraints or dislikes
- primary website CTA

If the user gives partial information, proceed with explicit assumptions instead of blocking unless the missing info makes the output unusable.

## Guardrails

- Treat outputs as **creative direction**, not final approved branding.
- Prefer one selected direction over endless exploration.
- Tie visual choices back to conversion, positioning, and website use.
- Keep outputs readable and implementation-oriented.
- Redact any secrets or sensitive information as `[REDACTED]`.

## Output structure

Return work in this order.

### 1. Brand brief

Keep it concise and practical:

- business summary
- target audience
- transformation / promise
- tone keywords
- visual constraints
- CTA / conversion goal

### 2. Three visual directions

For each direction include:

- direction name
- positioning angle
- vibe / emotional feel
- color palette direction
- typography direction
- imagery direction
- why it fits the business

The three directions should be meaningfully different, not cosmetic variants.

### 3. Chosen winner

Select one direction and explain:

- why it wins
- what it avoids
- why it is strongest for the website and business goal

### 4. Prompt pack

Generate prompts for at least:

- logo concept
- homepage hero image
- social promo image
- product/service explainer visual or banner

Prompts should follow the chosen direction and reuse the same palette, tone, and imagery logic.

### 5. Lightweight design system

Include:

- brand promise / direction summary
- color palette
- typography guidance
- component feel
- image style guidance
- CTA language guidance
- consistency rules

### 6. Website application workflow

Map the chosen direction into implementation steps for:

- homepage hero
- CTA
- service / offer cards
- social proof section
- founder/about section
- social or promo assets

## Recommended workflow

1. Clarify the business promise first.
2. Draft the brand brief.
3. Generate 3 sharp directions.
4. Force a winner.
5. Build the prompt pack from the winner.
6. Build the design system from the winner.
7. Produce the website application workflow.

## Quality bar

Good outputs are:

- specific
- visually coherent
- commercially relevant
- easy to apply to a homepage
- consistent across web and social assets

Weak outputs are:

- generic startup branding clichés
- too many options with no recommendation
- color/style notes disconnected from the business
- prompts that do not map back to website sections

## Response style

- Be concise but concrete.
- Use clean headings and bullets.
- Prefer practical language over design jargon.
- Make the result easy to copy into a website, prompt, or class worksheet.

## Classroom note

For AI Solo Company Class 7, keep repeating the key teaching boundary:

- generate three directions
- choose one winner
- apply it to the website
- do not treat the result as final approved branding
