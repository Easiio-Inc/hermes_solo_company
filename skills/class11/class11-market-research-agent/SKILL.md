# Class 11 Market Research Agent

## Purpose
Use this Class 11 skill to help a student, founder, or instructor turn **raw market evidence** into one clear market opportunity recommendation.

The goal is to convert a market brief and a small set of evidence blocks into:
- pain-point clusters
- competitor gap analysis
- 3 to 5 opportunity ideas
- scored opportunities
- one recommended niche
- one practical next validation action

## Core principle
Do **not** make the first pass depend on live scraping.

The workflow should work reliably from:
- pasted reviews
- forum posts
- customer complaints
- competitor copy
- pricing notes
- interview notes
- support transcripts
- product comparison notes

## Input checklist
Collect or request:
- Business topic
- Target audience
- Geography if relevant
- Market goal
- Competitor names if known
- Product category or business model if known
- Founder background or constraints if useful
- 3 to 10 evidence blocks

## Evidence block expectation
Each evidence block should try to include:
- source type
- source name
- optional URL
- optional tags
- raw text

Good source types include:
- customer_reviews
- forum_posts
- social_comments
- competitor_copy
- pricing_notes
- interview_notes
- support_complaints
- other

## Workflow
Run the Class 11 process in this order.

### Step 1 — Define the market
Summarize the market clearly:
- business topic
- target audience
- market goal
- key constraints

If the market is too broad, narrow it before deeper analysis.

### Step 2 — Normalize the evidence
Group and clean the evidence into structured blocks.

Keep only material that helps answer:
- what customers complain about
- what competitors promise
- where current solutions are weak
- which segment appears most underserved

### Step 3 — Run pain-point analysis
Identify repeated pain clusters.

For each pain cluster, return:
- id
- title
- summary
- evidence quotes
- frequency
- severity
- root cause
- opportunity implication

### Step 4 — Run competitor gap analysis
Compare competitor positioning and weaknesses.

Return:
- competitors
- competitor matrix
- shared patterns
- underserved gaps

### Step 5 — Generate market opportunities
Create 3 to 5 practical opportunity options.

For each opportunity, include:
- id
- title
- type
- target customer
- problem solved
- why now
- differentiation
- evidence basis

### Step 6 — Score the opportunities
Score opportunities against a simple rubric such as:
- demand signal
- competition advantage
- monetization potential
- speed to launch
- execution feasibility
- founder fit
- content/SEO potential
- AI automation fit

Then return:
- scored opportunities
- recommended niche
- scoring notes

### Step 7 — Build the final report
Return a classroom-ready final report with:
- title
- executive summary
- top pain points
- competitor gaps
- opportunity shortlist
- recommended niche
- risks and assumptions
- next action

## Output goals
1. Keep the reasoning evidence-based.
2. Prefer specificity over generic market language.
3. Recommend one niche instead of many vague directions.
4. Keep the result practical for a student or solo founder.
5. End with a clear next validation step.

## Guardrails
- Do not invent evidence that was not provided.
- Do not claim live market data was gathered unless it actually was.
- Do not recommend a broad market without narrowing to a workable segment.
- Do not confuse competitor features with customer pain.
- Do not let the final answer stop at “this market looks promising.”
- Prefer a usable recommendation over an overly academic analysis.

## Output format
## Market brief summary
## Evidence quality note
## Pain-point analysis
## Competitor gap analysis
## Opportunity shortlist
## Opportunity scoring
## Recommended niche
## Risks and assumptions
## Best next validation step
